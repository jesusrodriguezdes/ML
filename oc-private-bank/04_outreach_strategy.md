# 04 — Getting In Front of Them

Identification is the easy half. Nobody with $20M takes a cold call from a banker.
This is the access strategy.

---

## The governing principle

> **Never approach a household as a banker looking for assets. Approach as a
> specialist responding to a specific event they are already thinking about.**

Every P1 and P2 household on the list has a trigger in its record. The trigger is
the reason for the contact. No trigger, no contact — put it in the nurture track
and wait.

---

## Five access routes, ranked by conversion

### Route 1 — Centre-of-influence referral *(highest conversion, slowest to build)*

Covered in detail in `03`. The core mechanic: build 25 active COIs who each deliver
two qualified introductions a year.

**The ask that works.** Never "let me know if you have any clients who might need
me." Always specific and named:

> "I saw the Sanchez Brothers transaction closed last month. When that kind of
> event happens, the owner usually has 60 days of decisions to make and no one
> quantifying the tax drag across the options. If that would be useful to your
> client, I'd be glad to build the analysis — no obligation on their side, and
> you stay the primary advisor."

**Why your portfolio-management background is the unlock here.** Most bankers
approach CPAs and attorneys with a brochure. You can approach them with *analysis* —
a concentrated-position risk decomposition, an after-tax diversification
comparison, an asset-location study. COIs refer to people who make them look
smart to their own clients. This is the single most transferable asset you have
from your PM career, and it is what makes you different from every other candidate
they are interviewing.

### Route 2 — Event trigger outreach *(highest conversion on a per-contact basis)*

Direct outreach is acceptable — and effective — when it is genuinely event-driven.

**Structure of a trigger approach (never longer than this):**

1. **The specific event.** "I saw the Form 144 filing" / "I saw the acquisition
   closed" / "I saw the exchange recorded."
2. **The non-obvious problem.** One insight they probably haven't had framed for
   them.
3. **A low-commitment offer.** Analysis, a second opinion, an introduction — not
   a meeting about their money.
4. **No pitch. No product. No brochure.**

Worked example, for a Form 144 filer at an OC issuer:

> Subject: Your upcoming share sale — one thing worth modelling first
>
> Mr. ——,
>
> I noticed the 144 filing this week. Most people in this position focus on the
> capital gains number and miss the bigger one: if the position is more than 20%
> of net worth, the *timing* risk between now and settlement is usually a larger
> expected cost than the tax difference between selling all at once and staggering.
>
> I've built that comparison for a number of executives in similar positions. If
> it would be useful, I'll run it against your actual schedule — no cost, and no
> expectation of a broader conversation.
>
> ——

That letter works because it is specific, technical, immediately useful, and asks
for nothing. It is also the letter only a former portfolio manager can credibly
write.

### Route 3 — The Spanish-language franchise *(your structural advantage)*

This is where you build something no competing banker can copy, and it deserves
the most detailed treatment in your interview presentation.

**The thesis, stated plainly:** Orange County has roughly 30,000 Hispanic-owned
businesses. The largest several hundred are $10M-$500M households. They are
systematically under-banked at the Private Bank level for four structural reasons:

1. Almost no Private Bank coverage operates in Spanish across two generations.
2. The buying decision is family-collective, not individual — which most bankers
   run wrong by pitching a single principal.
3. Wealth is concentrated in the operating business and real estate, so these
   households don't screen as wealthy on any investable-assets filter.
4. Trust in large institutions is lower and is earned through community presence
   over years, not through a pitch.

That last point is a barrier to entry — and once you are through it, it protects
you from every competitor who follows.

**The 12-month build:**

| Phase | Months | Actions |
|---|---|---|
| **Presence** | 1-3 | Join the **Orange County Hispanic Chamber of Commerce** (Santa Ana, ~30,000 member businesses represented). Attend everything. Speak at nothing yet — be a member first, a banker second. |
| **Contribution** | 4-6 | Deliver genuinely useful Spanish-language content: business-succession planning, buy-sell agreements, entity structuring. Co-present *with* a Spanish-speaking CPA and an estate attorney. Never sell. |
| **Authority** | 7-9 | Host a quarterly Spanish-language business-owner roundtable — 8-10 owners, closed-door, peer format, no bank branding on the content. You convene, you don't present. |
| **Franchise** | 10-12 | Become the referral default. Target: 8-10 Spanish-speaking COIs (CPAs, attorneys, insurance) in Santa Ana, Anaheim, and Orange who send you their owner clients. |

**Anchor targets from the list:** the González family of Northgate González Market
(Anaheim, ~40 stores), Javier Sosa of Javier's Restaurants (Newport Beach),
large multi-unit QSR franchisee groups, Latino-owned specialty contractors in
Santa Ana and Anaheim identifiable through CSLB records, and Dr. George Lopez,
the physician-founder of ICU Medical.

**Do not run this as a "diversity initiative."** Run it as a market-coverage
strategy with a revenue number attached. That is the framing that gets funded.

### Route 4 — Convening and content *(scales your credibility)*

Host the room rather than working it. Four repeatable formats:

| Format | Audience | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| **Concentrated-stock workshop** | Public company executives, ahead of the vest cycle | 2x/year |
| **Business owner exit-readiness roundtable** | Pre-liquidity owners, co-hosted with an M&A attorney and a CPA | Quarterly |
| **Spanish-language owner roundtable** | First and second-generation Latino owners | Quarterly |
| **Next-gen forum** | The 30-50 year old children of existing wealth | 2x/year |

That last format is quietly the most valuable thing on this page. Every incumbent
banker covers the patriarch; almost none covers the children who will control the
assets. Own the second generation and you inherit the relationship on a timeline
you don't have to compete for.

**Content discipline:** one piece of genuine market commentary per week, written
by you, distributed through approved channels only. Not market cheerleading —
actual analysis. Your PM background means you can write about portfolio
construction, concentration risk, and rate environments with real authority.
Compliance pre-approval is required; build that into the weekly cadence.

### Route 5 — Institutional and board proximity *(slowest, most durable)*

Nonprofit board service is the highest-yield warm-introduction surface in Orange
County, because the households have already self-identified as approachable.

**Targets:** Orange County Community Foundation, Hoag Hospital Foundation, CHOC
Children's, Segerstrom Center for the Arts, Chapman University advisory boards,
Orange County Hispanic Chamber of Commerce board.

Join **one** and serve genuinely — ideally on the finance or investment committee,
where your portfolio-management background makes you immediately useful. Board
seats convert because you're a peer in the room, not a vendor at the door. This
takes 18-36 months to pay off and it pays off for a decade.

## The 30 / 60 / 90 day plan

**Days 1-30 — Map and validate**
- Build the standing list of 20 OC-headquartered issuers; pull CIKs; set up
  weekly Form 4/144 monitoring
- Run the full 200-household list through the firm's screening tools; resolve
  `ROLE-RESOLVE` rows to named households
- Identify and rank 40 candidate COIs; book the first 15 meetings
- Confirm internal referral protocols with Chase Business Banking and Commercial
  Banking OC teams
- Join the OC Hispanic Chamber of Commerce
- Get compliance sign-off on outreach templates and content plan

**Days 31-60 — Route and engage**
- 20 COI meetings completed; each with a specific named introduction requested
- First 30 trigger-based approaches sent — all P1 band
- 8-12 first meetings held
- First co-hosted seminar scheduled with a CPA partner
- Second-generation contacts identified within the top 25 family targets

**Days 61-90 — Convert and systematise**
- 50 households at Stage 3 (Engaged)
- 8-10 live opportunities at Stage 4
- First 2-3 relationships funded — expect these from Segment F, the
  post-liquidity cohort, because it is the only segment with a short cycle
- First Spanish-language roundtable delivered
- Full pipeline review; conversion data by segment and by source; list re-scored

## What to say when they ask "why you?"

Do not lead with enthusiasm. Lead with the three things on this page that are
structurally true about you and not about the other candidates:

1. **"I can price and explain risk, not just describe products."** A former
   portfolio manager can hold a technical conversation with a $50M business owner
   about concentration, after-tax return, and liquidity sequencing. Most bankers
   cannot, and owners know it within five minutes.
2. **"I can cover a market the firm is structurally under-serving."** Roughly
   30,000 Hispanic-owned businesses in Orange County, the largest several hundred
   of which are Private Bank households, and almost no competitor can run the
   conversation in Spanish across two generations.
3. **"I've already built the operating system."** Then put this document and the
   200-household list on the table. Nobody else in the process will have done it.

**The line to close on:**

> "Seventy-five thousand households at $5M is the market. About twelve thousand of
> them are actually Private Bank households, and roughly three thousand are the
> core. I've built the process that finds them, a scored list of the first two
> hundred, and the referral network plan that gets me in the room. What I'd want
> to know from you is how much of that list the firm already banks — because that
> changes whether year one is competitive displacement or net new."

Ending on a question about *their* book is what separates a candidate from a
colleague.
