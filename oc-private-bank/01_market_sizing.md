# 01 — Market Sizing: Stress-Testing the 75,000 Number

> **Interview note.** Do not repeat the 75,000 figure back to them uncritically.
> The single fastest way to signal you think like a banker rather than a candidate
> is to accept their number, then show what it actually means for a book. The
> number is *directionally right* and *strategically misleading*, and explaining
> why is the strongest opening you have.

---

## 1. Is 75,000 credible?

Orange County has roughly **1.06 million households** (≈3.15M residents ÷ ≈2.97
persons per household). 75,000 households at $5M+ net worth implies **7.1% of all
OC households**.

The national incidence of $5M+ *net worth* households is roughly **1.5%**
(≈2M of ≈132M households). So the claim puts Orange County at about **4.7x the
national rate**.

**Verdict: plausible, at the aggressive end of a defensible band.** For a county
with a median home value near $1.2M, a median household income roughly double the
national figure, and the wealth concentrations of Newport Coast, Corona del Mar,
Laguna Beach, Villa Park, and North Tustin, a 4-5x multiple is reasonable. Peer
ultra-affluent counties run 5-8%. A conservative independent estimate would land
at **60,000-70,000**; 75,000 is a defensible top-of-range figure.

## 2. Why the number is strategically misleading

The 75,000 is a **net worth** figure. Net worth includes the primary residence and
illiquid closely held business equity — neither of which a Private Bank can manage.

A household in Villa Park with a paid-off $1.6M home, $2.2M in a 401(k)/IRA, and
$1.4M of other assets is a genuine $5.2M net worth household. It is **not** a
J.P. Morgan Private Bank client. It is a Chase Private Client or J.P. Morgan
Wealth Management household.

Applying the standard adjustments:

| Filter | Est. OC households | Basis |
|---|---:|---|
| Net worth ≥ $5M (their number) | **75,000** | Accepted as stated |
| Less primary-residence equity → **investable** ≥ $5M | **~37,000** | OC $5M households hold ~30-45% of net worth in primary residence |
| **Investable ≥ $10M** — the realistic Private Bank threshold | **~12,000** | Standard ~3:1 step-down between $5M and $10M investable bands |
| Investable ≥ $25M — the PB core | **~3,200** | |
| Investable ≥ $100M | **~450** | Consistent with 12 OC names on the Forbes billionaires list |

*All figures are modelled estimates from public distribution benchmarks, not
survey counts. State the method, not just the number.*

**The takeaway to deliver in the room:**

> "Seventy-five thousand is right for net worth. But the Private Bank's addressable
> market in Orange County is closer to **twelve thousand households**, and about
> **three thousand** of those are the real core. That's not a discouraging number —
> it's a *targeting* number. Twelve thousand households is small enough that a
> disciplined banker can actually map it. It means the strategy is precision, not
> volume, and it means the entire game is competitive displacement."

## 3. The competitive reality

Twelve thousand households is a heavily contested pool. Orange County is one of
the most intensively covered wealth markets in the United States:

- **J.P. Morgan's own footprint** — the 2023 First Republic acquisition handed
  JPM a substantial existing OC client base and banker bench. Some of your
  target list is already inside the house. *Ask about this in the interview* —
  it determines whether you inherit relationships or compete for them.
- **Wirehouse incumbents** — Merrill, Morgan Stanley, and UBS all run large
  Newport Beach and Irvine complexes.
- **Trust and boutique specialists** — Northern Trust, Whittier Trust, Bessemer,
  City National.
- **Independent RIAs** — several hundred in the county, including large
  OC-headquartered firms such as Beacon Pointe.

**Implication:** almost no $10M+ OC household is unbanked. They are *under-served*
or *multi-banked*. Your plan should be explicit that you are competing for share
of wallet and for the *next* liquidity event, not discovering virgin households.

## 4. The whitespace — and why your profile fits it

Three pockets are genuinely under-covered:

1. **First-generation Latino business wealth.** Orange County has roughly 30,000
   Hispanic-owned businesses. The owners of the largest several hundred are
   $10M-$500M households. This segment is systematically under-banked at the
   Private Bank level: incumbent coverage is thin, trust in institutions is lower,
   the buying decision is family-collective rather than individual, and very few
   competing bankers can run the conversation in Spanish across two generations.
   **This is your single largest structural advantage and the plan is built on it.**

2. **Pre-liquidity operating businesses.** Owners whose wealth is trapped in the
   company are under-covered because they don't screen as wealthy on any
   investable-assets filter. They are reachable *before* the event, which is
   precisely when the relationship is won.

3. **The second generation.** Incumbent bankers cover the patriarch. The 35-55
   year old children who will control the assets within a decade are frequently
   unbanked and openly resentful of the inherited advisor.

## 5. Book economics and the funnel that justifies a 200-name list

Assumptions, stated plainly:

- Average new Private Bank relationship: **$8-15M** in assets under supervision
- Blended revenue: **~70bps** across advisory, lending spread, and deposits
- Realistic new relationships: **8-12 in year one** for a self-sourced banker

| | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| New relationships | 10 | 14 | 16 |
| Avg. relationship size | $8M | $10M | $12M |
| New AUS | $80M | $140M | $192M |
| Cumulative AUS | $80M | $220M | $412M |
| Approx. revenue @ 70bps | $560K | $1.5M | $2.9M |

**The funnel that produces those 10 closings:**

```
200 qualified target households      the working list
 ↓  25% converted to a first meeting
 50 first meetings
 ↓  40% progress to a live opportunity
 20 live opportunities
 ↓  50% close
 10 new relationships
```

This is why the deliverable is exactly **200 households**: at realistic private
banking conversion rates, a 200-name qualified list is precisely what one banker
needs to underwrite a credible year-one plan. It is not an arbitrary target — it
is the funnel inverted.

**Sales cycle expectations, which you should volunteer before they ask:**

| Segment | Typical cycle |
|---|---|
| Post-liquidity household (cash already in the bank) | 1-3 months |
| Pre-liquidity business owner | 9-18 months |
| Multi-generational family transitioning | 12-36 months |
| Professional practice partner | 4-9 months |

Two-thirds of a year-one book will come from the fastest two rows. The
pre-liquidity work planted in year one pays out in years two and three — which is
exactly the argument for hiring someone who will still be there.
